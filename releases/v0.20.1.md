# Housekeeping 0.20.1

The octopus stops breathing when nobody is looking. That is the whole release, and on a
laptop it is worth a quarter of a core, all day.

## What was wrong

Measured on this Mac on 30 October: a copy of Housekeeping sitting in the menu bar with no
window open was holding **about 28% of one core, continuously** — the top consumer on the
machine by its own panel's reading. Nothing was scanning, cleaning or downloading. She was
simply breathing.

Two things were doing it:

- **The menu bar octopus.** She was drawn into twenty-four template images at launch and
  then played back on a timer at twelve frames a second — a two-second loop, running from
  login to logout. Every frame is a status-bar image swap and a menu bar redraw, and the
  Mac never gets to settle between them.
- **The window's octopus.** A `TimelineView` redraws its whole body on every tick, and the
  main window's marks were running at twelve and twenty frames a second even with
  `working: false`. A window SwiftUI has closed but not torn down keeps its timelines
  firing, so a copy living in the menu bar was laying out and drawing a hidden window
  twenty times a second.

## What changed

Motion is now a budget (`MotionBudget`, in `Sources/UI/Motion.swift`). Decorative animation
runs while the app is active, while the mark's own window is actually on screen, and for a
couple of seconds at launch so the octopus reads as alive. Everywhere else the marks render
as a single still frame: same drawing, no clock, no timer, no layout work. A sleeping
display stops it outright.

Nothing about the app's behaviour changed. She still breathes while you are using the
window; she just no longer does it for an empty room.

Two smaller things came out of the same measurements:

- **The panel's octopus is still.** Every animated frame inside a popover makes AppKit
  re-solve the whole panel's Auto Layout — about 1.4% of a core per frame, measured, so a
  twelve-frames-a-second drawing in a 46-point corner was costing more than the rest of the
  panel put together. It is one frame now; the mark in the menu bar and the one in the
  window still move.
- **The panel no longer argues with itself about its height.** Reporting a height resized
  the popover, which laid it out again, which reported a height: a round trip the run loop
  ran as fast as it could. It now waits for the reports to stop and resizes once.

## Measured

Same Mac, same states, before and after:

| State | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Menu bar only, no window, app in the background | ~28% | **0.0%** |
| Main window open, app frontmost | ~28% | **~2.6%** |
| Menu bar panel open | ~21% | **~1.5%** |

That 28% was not a spike: it was the app's resting state for two and a half days.

## Downloads

- **Housekeeping-macOS-universal.zip:** Apple silicon and Intel in one app.
- **Housekeeping-macOS-arm64.zip:** Apple-silicon Macs only.
- **Housekeeping-macOS-x86_64.zip:** Intel Macs only.
- **SHA256SUMS.txt:** checksums for all three ZIPs.

Requires macOS 13 or later. The Bookbind screen needs Python 3 with `mutagen`
(`python3 -m pip install mutagen`) and `ffmpeg` on the Mac.

The app is ad-hoc signed. Developer ID signing and notarization have not been completed.
